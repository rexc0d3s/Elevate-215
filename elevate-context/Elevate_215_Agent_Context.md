# 5. WORKFLOW 1 — FUNDER REPORTING

## What is a funder report?
A funder report communicates what a funder's investment supported, what results were achieved, and what impact the investment had.

## Why this workflow matters
A funder may ask:

> **“What did my money do?”**

Stacy said she cannot answer this without pulling information from multiple people and sources.

## Current workflow

### Step 1 — Funder asks a question
The funder asks what their money did at a school.

Frequency documented:
- Approximately **8–10 times per year**
- More often in the fall during annual reviews

### Step 2 — Stacy gathers three main pieces

**Renée**
- School visit notes
- School performance/context

**Grant agreement**
- Original agreement
- Metrics
- Promises/requirements
- Terms

**Priya / Finance**
- Payment records
- What was actually paid

### Step 3 — Waiting and gathering
The information is not in one place.

Documented waiting estimates:
- School notes from Renée: **0–2 days**
- Grant agreement: **up to ½ day to locate**
- Payment information: **2–3 days**

### Step 4 — Stacy assembles the information
Once the pieces are available:
- Approximately **1 hour** of actual assembly
- Often a Word document or email draft
- Described as being built from scratch with no consistent template

### Step 5 — Stacy writes/checks the response
She:
- Checks the metric
- Makes sure information is current
- May contact the funder to confirm what is current
- Handles changes that may have occurred through calls or email

A documented problem:
- Metric changes can happen in calls or inboxes and may not become part of a durable shared record.

### Step 6 — Response goes to funder
Total process:
- Approximately **one week start-to-finish**

## Core friction
> **“Each of us owns a piece, but none of us own the whole story.”**

The information exists, but Stacy has to connect it.

### What the week represents
The week is not one week of continuous work.

It includes:
- Waiting
- Following up
- Gathering
- Finding documents
- Checking information
- Reconciling pieces
- Making sure the story is current

The actual assembly itself is about an hour once the information is available.

## Other documented costs
- Accuracy risk when metrics change in calls/email.
- Mismatched school names/spellings.
- No live portfolio view.
- Problems can surface late.
- Strong dependency on Stacy.
- If Stacy is unavailable, the process becomes significantly harder.

Stacy described the dependency with:
> **“I’m the process.”**

# 6. PROPOSED FUTURE STATE — FUNDER REPORTING

The proposed concept is not to remove people or human judgment.

The goal is to connect the pieces.

## Proposed information view

### Grants

- Metric
- Term
- Potential owners referenced: Yvette / Stacy

### Schools
- Metrics
- Results
- Notes
- Potential owners referenced: Renée / Marcus

### Payments
- Pledged
- Landed
- Coded
- Priya / QuickBooks

The proposed concept keeps information in its existing systems where possible and creates a connected view.

## Proposed automation concepts
The presentation/scope explored:
- Automated pulls
- A connected dashboard/view
- Freshness checks
- Automatic flags for old information
- Text/email alerts
- Rerouting when a person is unavailable
- Human review by Stacy

### Example: Renée unavailable
If school notes have not been updated within a defined period:
1. System flags the notes as potentially stale.
2. Relevant person/team receives an alert.
3. Team can update the notes.
4. If Renée is unavailable, the workflow can use the most recent available information where appropriate.
5. Review can be rerouted rather than stopping.

This is a **proposed future workflow**, not a currently implemented system.

### Example: Stacy unavailable
If Stacy is unavailable to review/approve:
- The request could be rerouted to the appropriate person.
- The goal is to prevent the workflow from simply sitting and waiting.

Do not assume who the backup approver is until Elevate confirms it.

## Human role stays
Stacy still:
- Reviews the information
- Uses judgment
- Decides what the story means
- Writes/approves the response

The proposed shift is:

**Today:** “Where is the information, and who do I need to ask?”

**Future:** “Here is the information I need. I can review the whole story and respond.”

# 19. SOLUTION PRINCIPLES

Any agent proposing a future workflow should follow these principles:

### Keep ownership where it belongs
Do not assume one person should own all data.

### Connect information instead of duplicating it
Where possible, create a view across existing information rather than forcing every team to change its source system.

### Surface stale information
The agent should identify when information may no longer be current.

### Alert instead of silently failing
If an expected update does not happen, surface it.

### Reroute when appropriate
If a person is unavailable, identify an approved alternative instead of allowing the process to stop.

### Keep humans in control
The agent should support review and decisions, not silently make high-impact organizational decisions.

### Make dependencies visible
Show:
- Who owns the information
- What is missing
- How old it is
- What action is needed
- Who should act next

### Reduce coordination work
The goal is not merely to move the same manual work into a prettier interface.

# 24. ONE-PARAGRAPH AGENT MEMORY

Elevate 215 is a Philadelphia education nonprofit focused on improving educational outcomes by increasing the number of high-quality schools. The discovery work found a recurring organizational pattern: important information exists, but it is distributed across people, systems, spreadsheets, documents, email, and personal knowledge, so staff often have to manually connect the pieces. The main scoped workflow is funder reporting, where Stacy may need Renée's school notes, a grant agreement, and Priya/Finance payment information before she can answer what a funder's money did; the actual assembly takes about an hour, but start-to-finish can take about a week because of waiting, gathering, follow-up, and checking. Other workflows include the board deck, which is manual but predictable; public/index data, which requires a twice-yearly manual refresh and can become stale; fundraising, where pledge information can be separated between a spreadsheet and CRM and a documented $75,000 grant required manual reallocation after funds landed incorrectly coded; and funder renewal work, where important relationship context was shown to live in individual knowledge when Yvette was unavailable. The proposed direction is to connect existing information, flag stale/missing updates, alert or reroute when people are unavailable, and preserve human review rather than replacing it. The central organizational goal is helping Elevate understand whether its investments are **“moving the needle for kids”** while reducing the manual work required to see **“the whole story.”**
